Colonizing the Southwest: Capitalism, Assimilation and Americanization

Alina Scott · 2018-04-09

Introduction

The focus of this lesson is to discuss the impact of western expansion and manifest destiny on Native peoples in the American Southwest. We will discuss the transformation of Native society on the backdrop of the Dawes Act, the land run, and attempts to populate the Southwest, in addition to the importance of giving voice to those silenced and offering providing accurate contextualization to primary source material.

This exercise, students will learn to pose questions of primary source documents by reading and analyzing "Indian Trader" catalogs, and comparing them to oral interviews with Navajo weavers. They conduct research to follow up these questions and use their findings to create an introduction to and annotations for the catalogs and acquisition documents.

The larger goal of this exercise is to introduce students to research and the importance to telling the stories of people who are still living and can be impacted by our work. While students are not required to formulate an original thesis, they learn to conduct independent research and synthesize primary and secondary sources.

Objectives


  • Read and transcribe handwritten documents

  • Pose critical research questions about a primary source

  • Question biases and influence of primary source authors

  • Find and evaluate appropriate secondary sources

  • Create well-researched annotations for places, people and terms mentioned in a primary source document


Context

Before utilizing the materials on "Woven into History" and trader catalogs, students will be introduced to such narratives as a genre. Using J. B. Moore's catalog as a jumping off point, we will discuss areas of interest relating to our contextual discussion of the result of the colonization of the American Southwest.

Assignment:

Utilizing the acquisition records, linked trader catalogs and students will be required to think deeply on biases, national identity, and systematic and environmental racism.

Part 1: Students will select one section of a trader catalog and one photograph of a weaver and blanket. The latter will serve as the inspiration for this project.


  • an introduction to the trader catalog providing relevant contextual information, a summary, and annotations


Part 2: Using additional primary and secondary information, students will draft an essay with:

  • A description of the textile with an explanation of symbolism and time period

  • Rewriting of “Weaver Profiles” with their voice using oral histories, imaginative questioning, and historical context from the Navajo perspective
