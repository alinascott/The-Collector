Turkey Red and the Making of an Oriental-Navajo Rug

Unpublished draft, 2018-03-09

The late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries witnessed a boom in the lucrative rug trade, the formations of which began much earlier. Although technological developments made rug and carpet production more inexpensive and accessible, handmade Navajo and Oriental rugs were still in high demand. By utilizing the catalogs, analyses and reports of traders and rug experts in the early twentieth century American southwest, this paper will discuss the theories that might suggest a relationship between Turkish and Navajo rugs. Further, the trader and expert records will be used to compare references to both groups. In this paper, I ask how traders understood the relationship between Oriental and Navajo rugs, if there is any empirical evidence in these accounts to suggest a relationship or influence, how religious ideologies shape the way traders marketed these rugs, and, finally, if there is any truth to a connection between Islamic prayer rugs and Navajo textiles.
